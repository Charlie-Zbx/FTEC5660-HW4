# Task 6: Reflection (draft for personal revision)

This is an AI-assisted draft. It describes possible commitments, not a record of personal experiences. I should revise it to reflect what I actually believe before submitting it.

If powerful personal AI agents become available to everyone, I would want to be someone who can decide what deserves attention and accept responsibility for those decisions. Being able to produce an answer quickly would matter less if almost everyone could do it. Choosing a worthwhile question, understanding who is affected by an answer, and being willing to correct a mistake would still matter to me.

My ambition to understand difficult ideas would survive, although its purpose might change. I would no longer need to treat every technical skill as a way to outperform other people. I would still want enough knowledge to question an agent's assumptions, notice when its evidence is weak, and explain my decisions to someone who has to live with their consequences. I would also want to build useful things. The measure of usefulness would be whether they improve someone's life, rather than how much output I can claim to have produced.

Beyond productivity and professional achievement, I would hope to be known as dependable and considerate. An agent might draft a thoughtful message, but I would still need to notice that a friend needs support and make time for them. It could propose a fair decision, but I would need to listen when someone says the decision has harmed them. I would want the people around me to trust that my attention is real and that I will follow through on my commitments.

There are struggles I would choose to keep. Learning a difficult subject without immediately asking for the solution can reveal what I do and do not understand. Having an uncomfortable conversation can require patience that a polished script cannot supply. Practising a physical or creative skill can be valuable even when the result is less impressive than an automated alternative. I would not preserve hardship simply because it is hard. I would preserve effort when the experience develops understanding, courage, or a relationship I care about.

The qualities I would most want to develop are judgment, intellectual honesty, empathy, and the ability to work with people whose priorities differ from mine. Technical literacy would remain useful, particularly the ability to test claims and distinguish convincing presentation from reliable evidence. Experiences outside a screen would matter too: making commitments, encountering unfamiliar communities, and seeing how decisions affect people in practice.

If my value is no longer defined mainly by what I can do, I would define myself partly through what I choose to care for and what I am willing to be accountable for. This would not remove the need for competence. It would give competence a purpose beyond status.

Tomorrow, I could start with three small changes: attempt a difficult problem before asking an agent for help, check one important AI-generated claim against its evidence, and give someone my undivided attention without trying to turn that time into a productivity gain. I would judge these habits by whether they help me become more thoughtful and trustworthy, rather than by how much faster they make me.
